Your practice session

Margaret came in for dizziness.

In a moment you'll speak with her. Your task is to take the dizziness seriously while using the encounter as an opening for The Wellness Dialogue.

Margaret Okafor
Age 58 · Administrative coordinator

Presenting complaint. Intermittent dizziness for about three weeks — mild but persistent enough that her daughter pushed her to come in.

Background. Generally healthy. Hasn't had a physical in three years. Past BP readings have been "a little high" but no one has discussed them with her. Family history of Type 2 diabetes. Overdue on screening.

Disposition. Practical, engaged, willing to talk. Will resist if the conversation drifts too far from her stated reason for the visit.

What you're practicing

The session is scored on five things.

Margaret is responding to your choices in real time. The scoring assesses how you handle the encounter — not whether you diagnose perfectly.

Take the chief complaint seriously.
Engage substantively with the dizziness before pivoting to anything else.
Surface the BP conversation naturally.
Introduce blood pressure as part of the dizziness workup, not as a separate topic.
Connect wellness questions to her concern.
Make the bridge between the immediate complaint and the longitudinal picture explicit.
Handle resistance without abandoning the dialogue.
When she pushes back, acknowledge and re-anchor — don't push through, don't retreat.
Close with a clear, agreed next step.
The encounter ends with Margaret committed to a specific forward action.
Before you begin
The conversation is live audio. Margaret will speak first.
You have five minutes. Speak naturally — there is no script.
Aim for a real conversation, not a perfect one. The point is practice.
"The training is successful when the practice disappears into the way you care."
