The practice

A defined way of engaging the patient at every encounter.

The Wellness Dialogue is a method, not a topic. It can be conducted in any visit, regardless of the presenting complaint.

The core principle
"The presenting complaint is the entry point — not the boundary."

Traditional encounters treat the chief complaint as the scope of the visit. The Wellness Dialogue treats it as the opening move of a broader conversation. The workup you'd conduct anyway becomes the natural surface for questions about blood pressure, diabetes, and screening — because those questions are connected to the workup, not appended to it.

The three checkpoints

Every encounter advances at least one.

You don't address all three at every visit. You listen for the opening each checkpoint offers, and use it when it appears.

Patterns that work
i
Connect the workup question to the complaint.

"Dizziness when you stand up can sometimes relate to blood pressure — let's check yours now." The wellness question is medically relevant to the visit, not adjacent to it.

ii
Translate clinical numbers into patient-actionable terms.

"Your reading is 145 over 90. Our target for you is under 140 over 80. That's the difference between staying on the path you're on and reducing your stroke risk." Numbers without translation are not actionable.

iii
Close with a concrete forward commitment.

The encounter ends with at least one specific next step the patient has agreed to. "Let's keep an eye on things" is not a forward commitment.

The pattern to watch for in yourself
×
Steamrolling patient resistance.

When the patient pushes back — "I came in for the dizziness, not for a physical" — the job is to acknowledge and re-anchor, not to push through. Steamrolling produces compliance in the room and disengagement after the visit. This is the failure mode most providers fall into, and the one role-play is designed to surface.
